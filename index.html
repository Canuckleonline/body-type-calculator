    Terms explained

    Body type, body shape and somatotype – what’s the difference?

    Body shape

    The outline of your figure – hourglass, pear, rectangle and so on. It is calculated from circumference measurements and is mainly used in fashion and pattern-making.

    Somatotype

    A model from the 1940s that groups builds into ectomorph (lean), mesomorph (muscular) and endomorph (rounder). Today it is used loosely in fitness to describe how a body tends to respond to training.

    Body type

    An everyday umbrella term. Depending on who uses it, it can mean either of the above – which is why this calculator offers both.

    The three somatotypes

    Ectomorph, mesomorph and endomorph

    Few people are a pure type. Think of the three as ends of a triangle – the quiz shows where you sit between them.

    Ectomorph

    Long limbs, narrow shoulders and hips, small joints. Often finds it hard to gain weight or muscle.

    • Prioritise strength training with compound lifts
    • Keep cardio moderate
    • Eat regular, sufficient meals

    Mesomorph

    Medium bone structure, broader shoulders and a naturally athletic look. Builds muscle relatively easily.

    • Mix strength and conditioning
    • Vary training to keep progressing
    • Recovery matters as much as effort

    Endomorph

    Wider frame, larger joints and a softer, rounder build. Tends to store energy more easily.

    • Combine strength training with regular activity
    • Daily movement like walking adds up
    • Focus on consistency over intensity

    Better results

    Five measuring mistakes that change your result

    1. Measuring over thick clothes. Jeans or a jumper can add an inch or more – measure over underwear or thin fabric.
    2. Pulling the tape tight. The tape should lie flat against the skin without pressing in.
    3. Holding your breath. Breathe out normally before you read the waist number.
    4. A tilted tape. Use a mirror to check the tape is level all the way around.
    5. Guessing the high hip. It sits about 7 inches (18 cm) below the natural waist, across the top of the hip bones.

    FAQ

    Questions about the body type calculator

    What is the difference between body type and body shape?

    Body shape describes the outline of your figure, such as hourglass or rectangle, and is calculated from measurements. Body type often refers to somatotype, which describes your build and how easily you gain fat or muscle: ectomorph, mesomorph or endomorph.

    Which body type is best?

    No body type is better than another. Each one simply responds a little differently to training and nutrition, and most people are a mix of two types.

    What body type are most men?

    Rectangle and trapezoid frames are the most common among men. A pronounced inverted triangle usually comes from a combination of naturally broad shoulders and upper-body training.

    Can I change my body type?

    Your bone structure stays the same, but training, nutrition and age can change how much muscle and fat you carry, which can move your measurements and your result over time.

    How accurate is the somatotype quiz?

    The quiz gives a general tendency based on self-assessment. A formal somatotype assessment, such as the Heath-Carter method, uses skinfolds, bone widths and girths measured by a trained professional.

    Is my data stored?

    No. All calculations happen in your browser and nothing you enter is sent or saved.
